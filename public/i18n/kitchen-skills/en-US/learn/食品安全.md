# Food Safety

## Poisoning

The following foods carry a risk of poisoning:

* Unripe green tomatoes
* Undercooked green beans (kidney beans), cowpeas (yardlong beans), and [jack beans](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%99%BD%E5%88%80%E8%B1%86)
* Sprouted potatoes (also regionally called yams), sweet potatoes (also called red sweet potatoes), and peanuts
* Improperly prepared/undercooked [daylily buds](https://zh.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%BB%84%E8%8A%B1%E8%8F%9C) can cause [colchicine](https://zh.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A7%8B%E6%B0%B4%E4%BB%99%E7%B4%A0) poisoning
* Uncooked soy milk
* Wood ear mushrooms soaked for too long (not just wood ear mushrooms: soaking any mushrooms for too long carries a risk of poisoning)
* Undercooked animal organs
* Unidentified or undercooked mushrooms (as the saying goes: "Red cap, white stem; eat them, and we'll all be lying on funeral boards together." Generally, the prettier the mushroom, the more dangerous it is.)
* ... (Feel free to add more.)

Storing and cooking acidic foods in aluminum containers for extended periods also carries a risk of poisoning. Examples include:

* Pickled mustard greens
* Dried bamboo shoots
* Tomato sauce
* Lemon juice
* Braised meat
* Soy sauce and vegetables pickled in sauce
* ... (Feel free to add more.)

## Allergies

The following are common food allergens (note: allergic reactions are generally lifelong):

Adults:

* Shrimp, crab, and other shellfish (eating seafood that is no longer fresh and has allowed bacteria to grow)
* Peanuts
* Tree nuts
* Fish

Children:

* Peanuts
* Tree nuts
* Eggs
* Milk (mainly intolerance to the A1 protein in cow's milk)
* Wheat and soybeans

## Salmonella Infection

Salmonella is more common in foods of animal origin. Vegetables may also contain Salmonella if contaminated with feces.

The following foods carry a risk of Salmonella infection:

* Eggs that are not fully cooked
* Meat that is not fully cooked
* Unpasteurized milk

## Aflatoxins

Aflatoxins are commonly produced by Aspergillus flavus, Aspergillus parasiticus, and several other molds in moldy grains and other staples, such as rice, beans, and peanuts. They are the most potent carcinogens known to date. They only begin to break down at temperatures above 280℃, so ordinary heating does not readily destroy their structure.

The following foods carry a risk of aflatoxin poisoning:

* Spoiled peanuts
* Spoiled rice
* Spoiled corn

Note that the foods listed above also include products made from them, such as rice noodles and cornmeal. Peanut sprouts (sprouted peanuts) produced under safe growing conditions can be considered safe to eat.

## 3-Nitropropionic Acid

3-Nitropropionic acid is produced by Arthrinium sacchari, a fungus commonly found growing on sugarcane and coconuts. Poisoning primarily causes damage to the central nervous system. Acute symptoms include vomiting, dizziness, episodes of convulsions, the eyes deviating to one side, coma, and even death. Lasting effects mainly involve damage to the extrapyramidal system, with symptoms such as flexion, twisting, spasms, limb rigidity, and reduced muscle tone at rest. There is currently no specific antidote for this toxin.

The following foods carry a risk of 3-nitropropionic acid poisoning:

* Sugarcane with a red center
* [Spoiled coconuts](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1w84y147TU)

Besides visual appearance, smell is also an important way to assess food. Spoiled plant foods often give off distinctive odors (such as fermented grain or sour smells). The best thing to do with food that smells spoiled is to throw it away.

## Parasites

Parasites can enter the human body through air, drinking water, food, and direct contact. If they enter the circulatory system, they can attack white blood cells, reach organs such as the lungs and liver, or block blood vessels or lymphatic vessels, causing diseases such as cirrhosis, portal hypertension, and elephantiasis. If a person becomes an intermediate host for the pork tapeworm, the parasites can even reach the eyes, heart, and brain, putting the person's life at risk.

It is best to make sure the following foods are fully cooked; otherwise, the corresponding parasites may remain in your body:

* Freshwater snails: rat lungworms
* Raw fish slices: liver flukes
* Swamp eels: Gnathostoma worms
* Bullfrogs: Spirometra mansoni larvae
* Pork: pork tapeworms
* The surface of beef (it can be eaten as long as the surface is cooked): beef tapeworms

## Safe Food Temperatures

Heating food to a sufficiently high temperature and holding it there for a certain amount of time can reduce, to some extent, the risk of bacteria and parasites surviving.
Different foods have different temperature requirements. Cooks should use a kitchen thermometer to measure the temperature at the center of the food.

Use a kitchen thermometer to measure the temperature.
Measure the temperature at the center of the food.

The following are industry-standard safe food temperatures:

|           | Whole Cuts                                            | Ground Meat  | Whole Bird  |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------|-------|-------|
| Pork      | 71°C                                           | 71°C  |       |
| Poultry      | 74°C                                           | 74°C  | 85°C  |
| Beef/Lamb | Medium-rare: 63°C; medium: 71°C; medium-well: 77°C         | 71°C  |       |
| Reheated leftovers | 74°C                                           |       |       |

Based on the latest 2011 standards from the [U.S. Department of Agriculture](https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/blog/cooking-meat-check-new-recommended-temperatures), safe cooking temperatures have been updated as follows:

|           | Whole Cuts                                            | Ground Meat  |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------|-------|
| Pork      | 63°C (rest for 3 minutes after removing from the heat)                 | 71°C  |
| Poultry      | 74°C                                           | 74°C  |
| Beef/Lamb | 63°C (rest for 3 minutes after removing from the heat)                 | 71°C  |
