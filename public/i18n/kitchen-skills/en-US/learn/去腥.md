# Removing Fishy and Gamey Odors

Removing fishy and gamey odors is a step in cooking.

This means removing fishy or gamey odors from meat, seafood, and other foods through methods including, but not limited to, adding seasonings and blanching.

**Fishy and gamey notes are part of the flavor of certain foods. Removing them too aggressively may strip the food of its flavor.**

There are many ways to remove these odors. Choose among them flexibly as you cook.

## Methods

### Adding Seasonings

Adding seasonings to ingredients is the simplest way to remove unwanted odors. For example, in most dishes that use beaten eggs ([Steamed Egg Custard](../../dishes/vegetable_dish/鸡蛋羹/鸡蛋羹.md), [Stir-Fried Tomatoes and Eggs](../../dishes/vegetable_dish/西红柿炒鸡蛋.md)), you can add salt, cooking wine, vinegar, or other seasonings while beating the eggs to remove their odor.

When cooking certain meats, you can add spices such as Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, bay leaves, cinnamon bark, fennel seeds, and chili peppers to the broth to remove unwanted odors.

Prepared mala hot pot base has an extremely strong aroma. Adding an appropriate amount while cooking is enough to mask the odors of most meats.

### Dipping Sauces

Some foods still have a strong fishy or gamey odor after cooking. You can prepare a dipping sauce to mask it as you eat.

Common dipping sauce ingredients include vinegar, soy sauce, sesame oil, chili bean paste, sweet wheat paste, sesame paste, peanut butter, fermented bean curd, salt, garlic, and ginger.

Everyone has their own preferences for dipping sauce combinations, so no examples are given here.

### Sizzling Aromatics in the Pan

When stir-frying, you can sizzle aromatics such as scallions, ginger, garlic, and dried chili peppers in the pan. High heat releases their aromatic compounds, which can mask unwanted odors to some extent and enhance the flavor of the finished dish.

### Blanching from Cold Water

Some animal ingredients, such as chicken, pig's trotters, and ribs, contain residual blood. If it is not removed, the finished dish will have some fishy or gamey odor.

When the ingredients are placed in cold water, the residual blood disperses into the water. As the temperature rises, proteins in the blood coagulate, and the blood that was dispersed in the water forms foam that floats to the surface. Simply skim off this foam with a spoon to remove the unwanted odors. The remaining clear broth can be used as the base for a stew or braise, and cooking can continue.

### Things to Keep in Mind

- When blanching, seasonings such as Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, cooking wine, and large scallions are often added to the pot to further improve odor removal
- Star anise has a strong aroma, so use an appropriate amount
- Sichuan peppercorns and extra-numbing Sichuan peppercorns are small and used in large quantities. Once added, they may remain in the pot or even in the finished dish. Wrap them in cheesecloth to make a spice bag, or use a food-grade stainless steel spice infuser, so they can be easily removed before the dish is finished
