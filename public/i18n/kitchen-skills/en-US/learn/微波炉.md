# Using a Microwave

## What Is a Microwave?

The microwave oven was invented in 1945 by [Percy LeBaron Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Spencer).

While working as a radar systems engineer, he noticed that a chocolate bar in his pocket started melting whenever the radar was turned on. This gave him the idea that led to his invention.

### How It Works

Microwaves are electromagnetic waves generated by a magnetron at a frequency of 2.45 billion hertz. This frequency causes water and oil molecules to vibrate and generate heat.

## Procedure

A microwave works very well for many cooking tasks.

High power is suitable for:

* [Cooking] Cooking vegetables
* [Cooking] Softening firm vegetables with a high water content (such as potatoes, onions, and artichokes)
* [Puffing] Puffing up snacks such as cream puffs, Indian papadams, and popcorn

Medium power is suitable for:

* [Cooking] Seafood (for example, [Microwaved Black Cod with Scallions and Ginger](../../dishes/aquatic/微波葱姜黑鳕鱼.md))
* [Cooking] Tenderizing meat
* [Dehydrating] Drying fruit and vegetable peels
* [Dehydrating] Making jerky
* [Frying] Crisping herbs
* [Frying] Softening leafy vegetables
* [Heating] Reheating leftovers

Low power is used for:

* [Thawing] Thawing food
* [Thawing] Melting butter and chocolate

## Precautions

* A microwave should not be used to heat water, as this can cause it to boil over suddenly and put your safety at risk.
* Never heat eggs in a microwave! The only exception is a **raw** egg **removed from its shell**, and you must pierce the yolk. Heating causes gases inside the egg to expand, leading to an explosion.
* Whatever your reason, even if you are curious, you should not microwave whole fruits (such as grapes, blueberries, or cherry tomatoes), as they may explode.
* Because microwaves have a wavelength of 12.2 cm, a microwave heats small objects more slowly than large ones. For very small ingredients, gather them together before heating.
* Microwaves penetrate only a few centimeters into food, so the outside can sometimes be very hot while the inside is still ice-cold. To address this, put the ingredients in a sealed bag with a little liquid, or place them in a bowl and cover it with plastic wrap. This allows enough steam to build up inside the container to compensate for the microwave's tendency to cook unevenly.
    * Tip: Watch out for escaping steam when opening the sealed bag.
* Microwaves can heat only substances containing liquid molecules, such as water and oil, so neither plastic wrap nor sealed bags are heated by microwaves.
* Metal reflects microwaves without being heated. Avoid containers with gold rims or metallic patterns, as uneven heating can cause them to crack. Be sure to use porcelain or glass containers, or a microwave-safe baking tray.
