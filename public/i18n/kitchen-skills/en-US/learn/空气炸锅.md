# Using an Air Fryer

## What Is an Air Fryer?

An air fryer is an electric cooking appliance that uses air instead of hot oil to cook food, producing an effect similar to deep-frying without the ingredients having to come into contact with oil.

### How It Works

An air fryer uses a heating element at the top to produce very hot air. The hot air circulates around the food by convection, rapidly heating the food's own fat and removing moisture. This produces a frying effect and a crisp texture similar to deep-fried food.

### Advantages

* Because no cooking oil needs to be added, it can **greatly reduce** your intake of cooking oil that is high in fat and calories.
* Rapidly circulating hot air dehydrates the food and turns its surface golden and crisp, leaving it crispy outside and tender inside.
* It is simple to use and beginner-friendly.

## Procedure

* Place the air fryer on a stable, flat, level, heat-resistant surface.
* Pull out the pan, place the ingredients in the fryer basket, and slide the pan back into the appliance.
* Change the preset temperature and turn the knob to adjust the cooking time.
* Once the cooking time is set, the appliance will start cooking. Wait for the timer to ring, indicating that cooking is complete.
* Empty all the food from the basket into a bowl or onto a plate. Be sure to remove the basket containing the ingredients from the pan, because **hot oil or fat may remain at the bottom of the pan**.

## Precautions

* When using an air fryer, avoid setting the temperature too high (try to keep it within 120℃, preferably no higher than 168℃) or cooking for too long (about 10 minutes), to avoid producing excessive amounts of the harmful substance [acrylamide](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%99%E7%83%AF%E9%85%B0%E8%83%BA).
* Cooking fewer starchy foods, such as potatoes, bread, and Chinese fried dough sticks, in an air fryer can reduce your intake of [acrylamide](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%99%E7%83%AF%E9%85%B0%E8%83%BA) accordingly. By comparison, air fryers are suitable for foods with a higher fat or water content, such as meat and vegetables.
* Do not block the air inlet on top or the air outlet at the back during use. Covering them with your hand may cause **burns from the hot air**.
* Temperatures can vary by as much as ±10℃ between air fryer brands. On your first attempt, reduce the cooking time by 10%, then adjust gradually.

### Key Techniques

1. **Preparation Matters**
    - Thaw meat completely and pat the surface dry (let steak/pork chops come to room temperature).
    - Frozen foods (fries/pizza) can be cooked directly, but shake/turn them more frequently.

2. **Preventing Sticking**
    - Line the basket with parchment paper or aluminum foil for foods that stick easily, such as fish/cake.
    - Line the bottom of the basket with onion/lemon slices to add flavor and keep the food separate from the juices.

3. **Controlling Browning**
    - Raise the temperature by 10-20℃ for the last 2-3 minutes for a crispier surface (for fries/chicken wings).
    - Cover the tops of egg tarts/cakes with aluminum foil to prevent excessive browning.

4. **Checking Doneness**
    - Meat: Press with chopsticks; firm meat is fully cooked, while soft, springy meat is partially cooked.
    - Cake: It is done when a toothpick inserted into the center comes out without any batter clinging to it.
