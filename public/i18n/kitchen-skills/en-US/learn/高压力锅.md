# Steaming (Rice)/Stewing (Using a Rice Cooker/Stovetop Pressure Cooker/Electric Pressure Cooker)

## What Is a Pressure Cooker?

A pressure cooker is essentially a regular pot with a locking, partially sealed lid. The lid has a valve that controls the pressure inside the pot.

### How It Works

A pressure cooker works by allowing steam to accumulate inside the pot, raising the pressure. As the pressure inside rises, so does the boiling point of water, allowing foods that contain water to cook at temperatures above 100 ℃.

### Advantages

* Because the actual cooking temperature inside a pressure cooker is higher, it can significantly shorten cooking times.
* The high temperature inside a pressure cooker promotes browning and caramelization, producing distinctive flavors.

## Procedure

* Place the ingredients and water in the inner pot, close the lid, **make sure the cooker is sealed**, and heat.
* For tough ingredients, such as tendons, a pressure cooker can make it relatively easy to cook them until tender for a better texture.
* Pressure cookers usually have a self-locking valve (float valve). During cooking, as the pressure inside increases, the self-locking valve activates and closes, preventing air from passing between the inside and outside so that pressure can build up. Once activated, the valve also locks the lid to prevent it from being forced open, providing a safety safeguard. During cooking, make sure the self-locking valve is not obstructed by any foreign objects so the pressure cooker can work properly.
* After switching to keep-warm mode, **release all the steam inside through the steam-release valve before opening the lid**.

### Precautions

* **Steam is very hot. Do not get close to the steam-release valve.**
* When cooking **liquid foods**, **do not release the pressure manually**, and beware of splattering (you can place the ingredients in a sealed jar or vacuum-sealed bag before cooking them in the pressure cooker).
* Manually releasing steam when cooking certain dishes (such as soups) **may affect their flavor and texture**.
* Before opening the lid, make sure all the steam has been released. Do not open the lid all the way at once, and especially **do not open it toward anyone**, to avoid steam burns.
* After cooking is finished, the self-locking valve will release as the pressure inside the cooker drops to match the pressure outside. You can use this as an indicator of whether the lid can be opened.
* A pressure cooker relies on the rubber sealing ring inside its lid to maintain a seal. For older pressure cookers, check whether the rubber sealing ring still works properly.
* Make sure the rubber ring is completely clean. Any particles caught in it may compromise the seal.
* Many pressure cookers have a maximum fill line. Ingredients and liquid should not go above this line. Too much food and liquid may cause steam to surge and block the steam-release valve, or cause excessive steam and water to spray out, making cleanup difficult.
* Even if a pressure cooker has no maximum fill line, it is best not to let the water level exceed 2/3 of the pot's height.
* **Do not use a pressure cooker to cook foods that foam easily, such as oats or dried noodles**. Foam may block the steam valve and pressure-release pipe.
* During cooking, if the pressure valve rises and releases steam or smoke, the pressure inside the cooker is too high, and the valve is releasing excess pressure for safety. Although the rich aroma of the escaping steam may be very pleasant, some of the food's flavor is lost, and excessive pressure may bend the locking slots on some types of pressure cookers. Therefore, when you see steam escaping, you can turn down the heat.
* Tip: Opening the lid from the side is a good option.
