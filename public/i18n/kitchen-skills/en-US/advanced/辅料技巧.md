# Tips for Aromatics and Supporting Ingredients

* The basic order for adding aromatics and supporting ingredients is as follows: ginger first, then scallions and garlic, then fresh chilies, then dried spices (star anise/Sichuan peppercorns/extra-numbing Sichuan peppercorns), and finally dried chilies. Depending on the dish, you can leave any ingredients you do not need out of this sequence.
* Ginger has the highest water content, which means we need more time to cook out its juices.
* If you are using scallion segments (it is best to give them a couple of smacks with a cleaver), I recommend adding them before the minced garlic. If you are using finely chopped scallions, you can add the scallions and garlic together. Note: The juices in scallion segments are harder to cook out.
* For stir-fried dishes, I recommend adding the dried spices before the ginger. This makes the oil more flavorful and the finished dish more fragrant. The reason for not doing this with stewed or covered, simmered dishes is that you may need to stir-fry a lot of aromatics and supporting ingredients, extending the cooking time so much that the dried spices turn black and bitter.
* Dried chilies go in last because the heat of the pan can easily turn them black. They only need a few seconds of stir-frying.
* Note: Whatever cuisine you like to cook, frying these aromatics and supporting ingredients over low heat until golden can give the entire dish a fragrance that seasonings alone cannot provide.

## When to Add Salt and How Much to Use

* For quick stir-fries, add salt first. Amount of salt = total weight of ingredients x0.9%;
* For meat dishes, add salt when the meat is eight-tenths cooked. Amount of salt = (total weight of ingredients + 30mL of juices) x(1～1.2%);
* For soups, add salt at the end. Amount of salt = initial amount of water x0.8%.
* Total daily salt intake is not recommended to exceed 5g. See [WHO](https://www.who.int/zh/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/salt-reduction).
