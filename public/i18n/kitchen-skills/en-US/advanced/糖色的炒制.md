# Making Caramel Coloring

Principle: Sugar melts at high temperatures and takes on different properties depending on how long it is heated.

The following example makes 200ml of caramel coloring.

## Water Method

1. Choose a pan **with a curved bottom** or **with a flat section that takes up less than 1/3 of the bottom**.
2. Add 8g of rock sugar, granulated white sugar, or fine soft white sugar to the pan. Rock sugar is best. If you want the caramel coloring to taste sweeter, you can add about 2g more.
3. Add 50ml of hot water at 70&deg;C to the pan.
4. Place the pan on the stove.
5. If using a gas stove, turn the heat down from high to its lowest setting, keeping the flame from extending beyond the area covered by the water. If using an induction cooktop, use its lowest setting and be ready to turn off the power at any time.
6. Stir the sugar and water constantly with a spatula. If using rock sugar, you can gently tap it with the back or edge of the spatula to crush it and help it melt faster. If the sugar starts changing color before it has melted, continue adding hot water to bring the volume back to 50ml.
7. Once all the sugar has melted, the sugar-water mixture will be thick and translucent with a whitish tint, like mica. Keep stirring at this point.
8. Keep stirring the sugar-water mixture as it gradually turns white.
9. Keep stirring the sugar-water mixture as it begins to turn light brown.
10. Keep stirring the sugar-water mixture until its brown color is very distinct.
11. Keep stirring the sugar-water mixture as it turns brown and develops a dense layer of bubbles.
12. Keep stirring the sugar-water mixture as it remains brown with a dense layer of bubbles, and larger bubbles appear among the small, closely packed bubbles.
13. Add 150ml of hot water at 70&deg;C to the pan. The liquid will now be a deep reddish brown, clear and transparent, with a faint caramel aroma.
14. Pour it into a bowl to yield 200ml of caramel coloring.

> [!Note]
> Additional information for step 13:  
>Do not substitute cold water for the hot water at 70&deg;C, because the low temperature will cause the sugar to solidify and stick to the pan.

## Oil Method(Recommended)

1. Same as the water method.
2. Same as the water method.
3. Add 10ml of room-temperature cooking oil to the pan.
4. Follow the water method, replacing the sugar-water mixture with a sugar-oil mixture and adding 190ml of hot water at the end. The remaining details are omitted.

## Syrup Stages and Their Uses

|Stage|Preparation Method|Uses (Examples)|
|:--:|:--:|:--:|
|Partially melted syrup|Water (oil) method, step 8|Sugar-frosted hawthorn berries|
|Fully melted syrup|Water (oil) method, step 9|Candied hawthorn skewers|
|Caramel|Let cool naturally after step 11 of the water (oil) method|Caramel|
|Caramel coloring|Water (oil) method, step 13|Caramel coloring|

* When using caramel coloring to cook food in relatively small pieces, such as red-braised pork, you can add the ingredients directly before step 13 and stir-fry quickly until there is no noticeable liquid pooled at the bottom of the pan. Then add water. This helps the food take on color faster.
