# How to Judge Oil Temperature, with Common Temperatures and Unit Conversions

* Oil temperature between 120&deg;C-140&deg;C: Suitable for soft-frying[^1] and velvet stir-frying[^2]. When you put chopsticks into the oil, almost no bubbles form around them. There is no bluish smoke or sound, and the oil is still.
* Oil temperature between 150&deg;C-160&deg;C: The ideal cooking temperature. When you put chopsticks into the oil, a few bubbles form around them. There is a little bluish smoke, and the oil moves from the edges toward the center.
* Oil temperature between 160&deg;C-180&deg;C: Suitable for frying food until browned and crisp. When you put chopsticks into the oil, a large amount of bluish smoke rises, while the surface of the oil is relatively still.
* (Note) It is best to buy an infrared thermometer for oil temperatures. Use thermometers with caution.

> In online video tutorials, oil temperatures described in "tenths" are relative to ten-tenths heat, which is 300&deg;C.
> That is, for n-tenths heat, T_n = 30n [degree Celsius]
> Temperature conversion formulas
> C = 5/9 *(F - 32)
> Or
> F = 9/5*C + 32
